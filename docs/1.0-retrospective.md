# Stockerly 1.0 — Retrospective

> **Historical document — written 2026-08-23, about a product that no longer exists.** Nothing
> here describes the current system; it is kept as the record of why the 2.0 looks the way it
> does. For what Stockerly is now, read [`vision/`](vision/) and
> [`architecture/adr/`](architecture/adr/).
>
> A single note distilling what Stockerly 1.0 and its closed beta were, why they
> failed, and the lessons carried into the 2.0 pivot. It replaces the sprint,
> archive, and audit history that was deleted after the pivot shipped — git
> preserves the full record. Current sources of truth: `docs/vision/` and
> `docs/architecture/adr/` (the pivot is [ADR-0010](architecture/adr/0010-pivot-to-self-hosted-single-user-tracker.md)).

## What 1.0 was

Stockerly 1.0 was a multi-user fintech web app — market trends, portfolios,
alerts, and earnings — built on Rails 8, Hotwire, PostgreSQL, and a pragmatic
DDD + hexagonal architecture across 6 bounded contexts. It carried the full
multi-user surface: registration, email verification, first-admin bootstrap,
invite codes, an API-key pool, user management, and admin ops.

Its intended audience was Adrian (dogfood) plus a **closed beta of ≤20 invited
friends**. It was built over 22 phases and later 12 sprints, reaching ~2,760
passing specs and 10 market-data gateways — a healthy codebase.

## Why it failed

The closed beta was run and failed on its secondary audience. Observed, not
assumed:

- Friends did not adopt it. They **did not know what to do** inside the app.
- They **could not read the indicators** (RSI, MA200, Fear & Greed).
- Loading trades and tokens was a *fastidio* — an annoying, tedious chore.

These were **product and UX failures — empty first-run, indicator illiteracy,
and data-entry friction — not architecture or code-quality failures.** The code
was sound; the audience was the problem. The "3 personas" of the original PRD
(active trader, casual investor, admin) were a fantasma. Adrian was always the
only real user.

## The pivot (2026-08-20, ADR-0010)

Pivot **in place, not a rewrite.** Stockerly became a **self-hosted, single-user
asset tracker** — a decision cockpit for one person (Adrian, dogfood), packaged
from day one so any technically capable person can stand it up with one command
and understand it without a manual.

- **Mobile-first, legibility-first.** The three failures are the product work:
  a seeded demo so first-run is never blank; inline `?`-tooltips plus one
  distilled signal so indicators are readable; snapshot + skip-history + smart
  CSV + Banxico-FX-auto-filled manual entry so data-entry stops being a chore.
- **MXN/USD correct** (historical FX at trade time) is the differentiator, not
  an "international feature".
- **No aggregators, ever, at this scale.** Plaid/Yodlee are a permanent
  non-goal — a cost trap that helped kill same-stack Maybe Finance.
- **Aggressive subtraction.** Most of `Administration` (invites, user
  management), the multi-user surface of `Identity` (registration, email
  verification, first-admin), `Notifications` down to in-app only, and the
  multi-user models were deleted. `MarketData` and `Trading` were kept and
  reinvested in. Fiscal scope (ISR, SAT) stays out.

The 2.0 cleanup shipped to production on 2026-08-23.

## Lessons carried forward

- **Build for the real user first.** "Self-hosted for anyone" is packaging
  discipline, not a mandate to build for a hypothetical community.
- **Document a personal trigger before any feature.** No trigger, no JTBD, no
  usage metric, no DoD → not built.
- **Match ceremony to complexity.** Don't wrap trivial CRUD in monads,
  contracts, and events.
- **Audit usage before extending a feature.** Ask "did anyone actually use it?"
  before layering more on top.
- **Retros are mandatory.** A sprint that closes without asking what worked and
  what didn't repeats its own mistakes.
